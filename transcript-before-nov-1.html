Nov 1 checklist

Homeschool transcript for college applications: what to send before Nov 1

Early Action deadlines land November 1 and 15, FAFSA opens October 1, and Regular Decision runs January 1 to 15 (check each college’s own dates). If your senior’s transcript is not built yet, this is the month. Here is what an admissions reader is checking, and the free samples to copy the structure from.

Colleges accept parent-issued transcripts.

That is the normal path for homeschool applicants, and admissions offices read thousands of them a year. There is no seal you are missing. What a reader checks, in the couple of minutes they spend on the file, is whether the document is complete and consistent.

The five things a reader checks

  1. Credits that add up. State what a credit means in your homeschool (most families use 120-180 hours, or completion of a text) on the transcript itself.
  2. A stated grading scale. 4.00 unweighted is the easiest to read; if you weight anything, say exactly what and why, in one line.
  3. Course titles that describe real work. “English I: Literature & Composition” beats “English.”
  4. Course descriptions behind the titles. A paragraph per course: what was read or used, what the student produced, how it was assessed.
  5. Consistency. Same student name, same dates, same scale everywhere; the GPA matches the courses listed above it.

What gets a file set aside.

Not a bad transcript, an ambiguous one: the reader cannot tell what a course was, follows up, and the follow-up costs you weeks at the worst possible moment.

If your records are scattered.

Photos of reading lists, curriculum receipts, a co-op syllabus and your own memory are enough to reconstruct a year. Where there is a real gap, leave it out rather than inventing something.

The dates (verify with each college).

October 1: FAFSA opens. November 1 and November 15: most Early Action and Early Decision deadlines. January 1-15: most Regular Decision deadlines. Transcripts, course descriptions and a school profile are usually uploaded by the parent through the application platform.

Questions

Are parent-issued transcripts really accepted?

Yes; it is the normal path. There is no seal to be missing.

Is a $20-$40 transcript tool the same thing?

For clean records, a transcript-maker tool ($20-$40 a year) produces a fine transcript, and you should use one if that is all you need. What those tools do not do is write the course descriptions, set a defensible grading scale and credit definition, build the four-year plan or the counselor-letter framework, or have a person read every line before it goes to a college.
